FSS-Enhanced Multiband Rectenna for Ambient RF Energy Harvesting

1. Project Overview

This project focuses on the design and simulation of an FSS-enhanced multiband rectenna capable of harvesting ambient Radio Frequency (RF) energy and converting it into usable DC electrical energy.

The proposed system combines a circular planar monopole antenna, Frequency Selective Surface (FSS), impedance matching network, and a Schottky diode-based voltage doubler rectifier.

The design targets two important frequency bands:

- 0.9 GHz: Cellular/RFID applications
- 2.4 GHz: Wi-Fi/ISM applications

The main aim is to explore an efficient and compact RF energy harvesting solution for low-power electronic devices and wireless sensor networks.

2. Project Objectives

- Design a compact multiband RF receiving antenna.
- Enhance antenna performance using Frequency Selective Surface (FSS) technology.
- Capture ambient RF signals from available wireless sources.
- Convert received RF energy into DC electrical energy.
- Explore the potential of battery-less or low-power IoT applications.

3. System Architecture

The proposed system consists of four major stages:

1. RF Energy Reception: The antenna receives ambient electromagnetic waves.
2. FSS Enhancement: The Frequency Selective Surface is designed to modify the antenna's electromagnetic response and improve its performance.
3. Impedance Matching and Rectification: An L-network matching circuit and Schottky diode-based voltage doubler are used for RF-to-DC conversion.
4. DC Output: The rectified output is filtered and supplied to a suitable low-power load.

4. Working Principle

The rectenna operates by receiving electromagnetic energy available in the surrounding environment.

The circular planar monopole antenna captures RF signals at the targeted frequency bands. The FSS structure is incorporated to improve the antenna's frequency response and radiation characteristics.

The received RF signal is then transferred through an impedance matching network to the rectifier circuit.

A nonlinear voltage doubler using Schottky diodes converts the RF signal into DC voltage. Filtering capacitors help smooth the output before it is delivered to a low-power load.
